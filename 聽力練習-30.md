# 聽力練習 · 30 篇

虛構場景，用來練聽力。不是投資建議，也不是法律或房產建議。

前 20 篇約 **400–600 個英文詞**，分成簡單與中等。後 10 篇約 **800–1000 個英文詞**，句子更長，說話的人更多，理由要聽完才站得住。詞數在每篇標題下，以英文詞計算。

這些故事是用站上已有的主題重新拼出來的：生活與飲食、職場、不動產、CFA 財金、表達力、F1。人名和港口城都是新的，不是舊課逐字搬過來。標了名字的對白，是同事、主管、客戶、工班或貸款員在說話。沒有名字標示的段落，是敘事，適合一個人朗讀。

## 怎麼用

1. 先讀「題旨」，蓋住英文稿。
2. 聽或朗讀一遍，抓時間順序、誰做了決定、理由是什麼。
3. 再用下面三題檢查。簡單篇先對細節；中等篇對立場；強化篇對取捨。
4. 對過之後再看「中文翻譯」。

## 01　The Second Alarm

- 難度：簡單
- 詞數：520
- 主題：生活節奏 × 讀書計畫
- 聽力焦點：一天的順序，以及「先做完一件小事」
- 題旨：Owen 用第二個鬧鐘開始一天。他上午在咖啡館上班，下午讀道德章節，晚上因為下雨沒去散步，但把明天的計畫寫下來。

### 聽力稿

Owen set his alarm for six, then a second one for six-ten, because he knew what the first one would do. It would ring, he would turn it off, and the room would go quiet again. The second alarm was the one that got him to the kitchen.

He did not make a big breakfast. He boiled water, poured it over oats, and cut a banana into the bowl. While the oats cooled, he opened the notebook he kept by the window. The left page was for the day. The right page was for the week. On the left page he wrote three lines: a café shift from nine to one, ethics reading from three to five, and a walk after dinner if the rain stopped.

The café was six blocks away, past the harbor market. On clear mornings he walked. On wet mornings he took the bus and stood near the door so he could get off quickly. That Tuesday was wet. He held his bag against his chest and watched the gray water between the boats. He was not unhappy. He had slept. He had eaten. The hard part of the day had not started, and that felt like enough.

At the café, Lena was already behind the counter. She pointed at the clock, then at the empty tables.

**Lena:** We open in twenty minutes. Can you wipe the window tables first? The night rain left marks.

Owen nodded. He liked tasks with a clear end. Wipe the tables. Fill the water jars. Write the soup of the day on the board. Those jobs ended. The ethics chapter did not end so cleanly.

During a quiet hour he stood by the pastry case and listened to two regulars. One man said he was going to sell his car. The other asked if he was sure, because he used the car every day. The first man said he was sure. Parking near his new office cost more than the car was worth. Owen smiled into a cup he was drying. People made plans out loud here. He made his plans in a notebook, but the shape was the same: a reason, a cost, and a next step.

At one o'clock he hung up his apron. Lena asked if he would come at the same time tomorrow. He said yes. On the walk toward the library the rain had thinned. He did not go back to the apartment, because the apartment was noisy in the afternoon. The library had a table by a window where his phone did not tempt him as much. He read until five. He did not finish the chapter. He finished the section on putting the client's interest first, and he wrote one sentence in the notebook: if he was not sure who benefited, he would ask before he spoke.

In the evening he cooked rice and eggs. He crossed out "walk" and wrote "rain," then he added a smaller line under it: walk tomorrow. The second alarm had done its job. The day had a beginning, a middle, and a place to start again.

### 中文翻譯

Owen 把鬧鐘設在六點，又設六點十分那一個，因為他太清楚第一個會怎麼樣：響了，他關掉，房間又靜下來。真正把他逼進廚房的，是第二個鬧鐘。

他沒做豐盛早餐，只燒水沖燕麥，切一根香蕉進碗。等燕麥涼一點，他打開窗邊的筆記本：左頁記今天，右頁記這週。左頁他寫三行——九點到一點咖啡館的班、三點到五點讀倫理、晚餐後若雨停就去散步。

咖啡館在六個街區外，要經過港口市場。天晴就走路，下雨就搭公車，站在門邊好快下車。那個星期二一路濕漉漉的。他把包抱在胸前，看船與船之間灰灰的水。他不算難過：睡飽了，也吃過了，一天最硬的部分還沒開始，這樣就夠了。

到店時 Lena 已在櫃台後。她指指時鐘，又指指空桌。

**Lena：** 還二十分鐘開門。你先擦靠窗那幾桌好嗎？夜裡的雨在水痕上。

Owen 點頭。他喜歡有清楚終點的事：擦桌、補水瓶、把今日湯寫上黑板，做完就完。倫理那一章可沒這麼乾淨。

客少的那一小時，他站在點心櫃旁聽兩個常客聊天。一個說要賣車，另一個問確定嗎，他明明每天都開。第一個還是說確定：新辦公室附近停車費，比車本身還貴。Owen 對著手裡擦的杯子笑了笑。這裡的人把打算說出來；他寫在筆記本裡，道理一樣：理由、成本、下一步。

一點整他掛上圍裙。Lena 問明天是否同一時間來，他說好。往圖書館走時雨小了。他沒回公寓——下午那裡太吵。圖書館窗邊有張桌子，手機比較不會拖他分心。他讀到五點，整章沒讀完，但「客戶利益優先」那一節讀完了；筆記本加一句：若不確定誰受益，開口前先問。

晚上他煮飯加蛋，把「散步」劃掉改寫「雨」，底下再補一行：明天再走。第二個鬧鐘盡了本分。這一天有開頭、有中間，也知道明天從哪接起。

### 聽完再問

1. Why does Owen set two alarms?
   為什麼 Owen 要設兩個鬧鐘？
2. What three things does he plan for Tuesday?
   星期二他計畫做哪三件事？
3. What sentence does he write after reading?
   讀完之後他在筆記本寫下哪一句？

## 02　Soup for Six

- 難度：簡單
- 詞數：466
- 主題：料理步驟 × 招待朋友
- 聽力焦點：先後順序，first / then / while
- 題旨：Lena 週日為六個人煮湯。她先列清單、再備料，湯滾著的時候擦桌子。朋友晚到，她沒有生氣，因為湯可以再熱。

### 聽力稿

On Sunday Lena did not open the café. She opened her own small kitchen instead. Six friends were coming at six, and she had promised soup, bread, and one bowl of fruit. She did not want the evening to become a performance. She wanted people to sit, eat, and talk without watching her rush.

She started with a list, because a list kept her from walking to the market twice. Carrots, onions, a can of tomatoes, a bag of lentils, parsley, and a loaf of bread that could go into the oven later. She already had salt, oil, and garlic. At the market she bought the bread last, so it would not be crushed under the vegetables.

Back home she washed her hands, then she set a pot on the stove before she cut anything. Her mother had taught her that. If the pot was ready, the cooking had begun, and she was less likely to stop and answer messages. She heated a spoon of oil. The onion went in first and turned soft. The carrots followed. She added the lentils and the tomatoes, then enough water to cover everything by two fingers. When the pot started to boil, she turned the heat down. Lentils did not like a violent boil. They liked time.

While the soup cooked, she cleared the table. She moved a stack of café schedules onto a chair. She put out six bowls, but only four spoons at first, because she could not find the other two. They were in a jar by the sink, washed that morning and forgotten. She laughed at herself and set them out.

At half past five she tasted the soup. It needed salt and a little acid. She did not have lemon, so she added a small splash of vinegar and tasted again. Better. She tore the parsley with her hands and left it on a plate, to add at the end so it would stay green.

The first friend arrived on time. The others came late, wet from a bus stop without a roof. Lena did not scold them. The soup could wait on low heat. She cut the bread, put the fruit in the center of the table, and told people to serve themselves. Someone asked for the recipe. She said there was no real recipe, only an order: onion, carrot, lentil, tomato, time, then salt at the end.

They talked about work, a broken umbrella, and a movie no one had finished. Lena listened more than she spoke. When the pot was empty, she was tired in a clean way. The dishes could wait ten minutes. The good part had already happened. Six people had eaten, and nobody had needed her to be perfect. They had only needed the soup to be hot.

### 中文翻譯

星期天 Lena 沒開店，改開自己那間小廚房。六個朋友六點要來，她答應了湯、麵包和一盆水果。她不想把晚上搞成表演，只希望大家坐下來吃、聊，不用盯著她手忙腳亂。

她先列清單，免得跑兩趟市場：紅蘿蔔、洋蔥、一罐番茄、扁豆、巴西里，還有一條稍晚再進烤箱的麵包。鹽、油、蒜家裡都有。麵包她留到最後才買，免得被菜壓扁。

回家先洗手，切菜前就把鍋放上爐——母親教她的：鍋一就位，就算開工，比較不會半途去回訊息。熱一匙油，洋蔥先下炒軟，再紅蘿蔔，接著扁豆和番茄，加水蓋過食材約兩指。滾了就把火轉小；扁豆受不了大火狂滾，得慢慢來。

湯在煮，她清桌子，把一疊咖啡館班表挪到椅子上。六個碗都擺了，湯匙先只放四支——另外兩支找不到，原在水槽邊罐裡，早上洗好就忘了。她笑自己，把湯匙補齊。

五點半嚐湯，缺鹽也缺一點酸。沒檸檬，就滴少許醋再嚐，好多了。巴西里用手撕，盤上留著，上桌前再加，綠才會留住。

第一個朋友準時到；其他人遲到，在沒棚的公車站淋濕。Lena 沒罵人，湯可以小火等。她切麵包，水果放桌中央，叫大家自己盛。有人問食譜，她說沒有標準食譜，只有順序：洋蔥、紅蘿蔔、扁豆、番茄、時間，最後才鹽。

他們聊工作、壞掉的傘、一部誰都沒看完的電影。Lena 多半在聽。鍋空了，她累但心裡踏實；碗盤可以晚十分鐘再洗。該有的都有了：六個人吃飽，沒人要求她完美，只要湯是熱的。

### 聽完再問

1. What does Lena buy last at the market, and why?
   Lena 在市場最後買什麼？為什麼？
2. Why does she turn the heat down after the pot boils?
   湯滾了之後，她為什麼把火轉小？
3. How does she react when her friends arrive late?
   朋友遲到時，她怎麼反應？

## 03　The Empty Living Room

- 難度：簡單
- 詞數：464
- 主題：看房
- 聽力焦點：房間位置，there is / there are，以及還不能決定
- 題旨：Noor 第一次跟房仲 Priya 看一間空屋。客廳採光好，但廚房舊、臥室靠馬路。她沒有當天出價，只把三個問題寫進手機。

### 聽力稿

The apartment was empty, which made it look larger and also a little sad. Noor stood in the living room with her hands in her coat pockets. Sun came in through a wide window and drew a bright square on the floor. She could imagine a table there. She could also imagine the table getting cold in winter, because the window was only one sheet of glass.

Priya, the agent, did not rush her. She opened the kitchen door and waited.

**Priya:** There is a gas stove, and there are three cabinets that close properly. The fourth one needs a new hinge. The owner knows. It is written in the listing notes.

Noor opened the fourth cabinet. The door dropped a little. She shut it gently. The sink was clean. The tiles behind it were old but not cracked. She turned on the tap. The water ran clear, then she turned it off. A small thing, but she had read that she should listen to pipes, not only look at paint.

The bedroom faced the street. When a bus passed, the glass hummed. Priya said the building was on a bus route, which was useful in the morning and noisy at night. Noor sat on the built-in ledge under the window and counted ten seconds of quiet between two buses. It was not terrible. It was also not the silence she had at her friend's house on the hill.

There was one bathroom, with a window that opened onto a light well. There was a closet in the hall, deep enough for a bicycle if she took the front wheel off. There was no balcony. Priya said the roof was shared, and residents could go up if they signed a short form. Noor liked the idea of air above the street. She did not like the idea of signing anything on the first visit.

They walked the hallway again. The floor was wood, scratched near the door where people had dragged boxes. Priya said the scratches were normal and would not change the price much. Noor nodded, but she was not ready to talk about price. She was still trying to picture a Tuesday night: cooking, a call to her sister, the bus outside.

**Priya:** You do not have to decide today. Open houses feel fast. A good decision is usually slower.

Noor thanked her. On the sidewalk she opened her phone and wrote three questions. What does the heating cost in January? Can the bedroom window take a thicker curtain? Is the roof really open after eight? She could ask them tomorrow. Today she only needed to remember the bright square on the floor, and the sound of the bus, and the fact that she was allowed to leave without a promise.

### 中文翻譯

公寓空著，看起來更大，也略帶空虛。Noor 站在客廳，手插在外套口袋。寬窗透進陽光，在地板照出一塊亮光。她想像那裡放一張桌子，也想像冬天桌面會冷——那扇窗只有單層玻璃。

房仲 Priya 不催她，打開廚房門，等著。

**Priya：** 有瓦斯爐，三個櫃門關得緊；第四個要換鉸鏈，房東知道，物件備註裡有寫。

Noor 拉開第四個櫃，門往下垂了一點，她輕輕關上。水槽乾淨，磁磚舊但沒裂。她開水龍頭，水先清亮地流，再關上。小事，但她讀過：別只看油漆，也要聽管子。

臥室朝街，公車一過玻璃就嗡嗡響。Priya 說這棟在公車線上，早上方便、晚上吵。Noor 坐在窗下窗檯座，數兩班公車之間約十秒的安靜——不算糟，但比不上山上朋友家那種靜。

一間浴室，窗朝採光井；走廊有深衣櫃，拆前輪腳踏車也塞得進。沒陽台；Priya 說屋頂共用，住戶簽個簡短表格就能上去。Noor 喜歡「街上有空氣」這念頭，但不喜歡第一次看房就要簽任何東西。

他們又走一遍走廊。木地板在門邊有拖箱刮痕。Priya 說很正常，不太影響出價。Noor 點頭，還沒準備談價，心裡還在演一個星期二晚上：煮飯、打給姊姊、窗外公車聲。

**Farid:** Pit means they come in for tires or a repair. Soft tires are quicker and die sooner. Hard tires are slower and last longer. If someone pits early, they hope the new tires will give the time back.

## 07　The Promise on Page One

**Helen:** That prompt might be from a paid bank. Even if the client is fake, the words may not be ours to copy.

At three-forty she was still upstairs. A recorded voice said the galleries would close at four. She had planned a gift-shop stop and a slow walk to the station. Both plans died. She used the restroom, filled her bottle, and left without the postcard she had wanted. The walk to the station took sixteen minutes at a fast pace. The board showed her five-ten train, and also a four-fifty that she had already missed. She was not late yet. She was close enough to feel her pulse in her ears.

She made the five-ten with four minutes to spare. The carriage was warm. She ate the last of the apple and wrote times in her notebook, the way Owen did, though she did not know his habit then. Train, museum, bread, museum, run, train. Next to the soup price she wrote "no." Next to the chair card she wrote "dry quickly." Those were the two facts she wanted to keep.

A man across the aisle asked if the design museum was worth it. She said yes, if he liked ordinary objects taken seriously, and if he watched the clock. ## 09　The Kitchen Wall

- 難度：簡單
- 詞數：450
- 主題：裝修 × 工班

### 聽力稿

The kitchen was the size of a large closet with ambitions. Lena wanted new tiles on one wall before she rented the place to a tenant. Priya had suggested a contractor she had used on two other small jobs. Chris arrived with a pencil behind his ear and dust on his boots. He did not sit. He looked at the wall, the tap, and the floor.

**Chris:** First we turn the water off. Then we protect the floor. Then we take the old tiles down. If we start with glue and pretty squares, we will trap whatever is soft behind them.

Lena asked if the wall was soft. Chris pressed a corner with his thumb. It was firm. He said firm was good, and he still wanted to see the pipe, because the tap had a green mark where water had wept and dried. A small weep could become a large bill if you covered it.

Priya translated nothing. Both people were speaking plain language. She only kept the calendar.

**Priya:** The tenant hopes to move in on the first. Can this wall be finished before that?

Chris counted on his fingers, not as a performance, but as a man checking drying time. Take down, half a day. Let the wall dry, a day if the weather helped. New board if they found damage, another day. Tiles, a day. Grout that should not be rushed, a day. He looked at Lena.

**Chris:** If today is Wednesday, finished and safe is next Wednesday. Before the first, yes. Before this weekend, no. I can do the wall you see and stop. I cannot also move the sink and promise Friday.

Lena wanted the sink moved. She also wanted a tenant who did not start life in a wet kitchen. She stood quietly, which Priya recognized as her thinking face, the same face she used when she priced a soup.

**Lena:** Do the wall. Leave the sink where it is. I will tell the tenant the sink is old but honest. Can you start Monday?

Chris said Monday if the water could be off from eight to noon. Lena said she would warn the upstairs neighbor, because they shared a stack. Priya wrote the sentence in the file: wall only, sink stays, water off Monday morning, no weekend promise.

Before he left, Chris tapped the loose burner he noticed near the stove. He said it was not part of the job, and it was a five-minute fix if someone asked. Lena said she would ask. She liked people who named the edge of their work. A promise that knew its edge was a promise she could repeat to a tenant without blushing.

Lena 問牆軟不軟。Chris 拇指按一角——硬的。他說硬是好事，仍要看管子：水龍頭有綠痕，是水滲過又乾的痕跡；小滲若蓋住，日後可能是大帳單。

## 10　Three Boxes on the Calendar

- 難度：簡單
- 詞數：430
- 主題：目標 × 時間管理
- 聽力焦點：三個目標如何排進一週，而不是願望清單
- 題旨：Owen 把本月目標收成三格：班表、讀書、睡眠。他刪掉「每天運動一小時」，改成下班走路。週日他只檢查有沒有做到，不加重新目標。

### 聽力稿

On the last Sunday of the month, Owen drew three boxes on the right-hand page of his notebook. He had learned that a long list made him feel busy and then guilty. Three boxes made him choose. The first box was work. The second was study. The third was sleep. He wrote them in that order, then stared at the third, because sleep was the one he usually treated as leftover time.

Work was the café and two short office shifts Dana had offered him, labeling charts, not touching client numbers. He wrote the hours. They fit. Study was ten hours for ethics and two hours for a quantitative chapter he kept postponing. He put the quantitative hour on Wednesday night, when the café closed early. He put ethics on the library afternoons he already knew how to use.

Sleep was a number, not a mood. He wanted seven hours on work nights. That meant lights out at eleven if the alarm stayed at six. He looked at the line and almost bargained with it. A film with Farid would push bedtime to twelve-thirty. He wrote the film on Saturday instead, when he could sleep later. The box did not care about his feelings. It cared about whether Tuesday would still have a kind morning.

He had wanted a fourth box called "exercise every day for an hour." He crossed it out. An hour every day was a speech, not a plan. He wrote "walk home from the bus when it is not pouring" under the work box. That was real. It used a road he already stood on.

Dana had said, in the short Monday meeting, that a block should be named in one sentence. Owen tried that on himself. The block was not laziness. The block was a calendar that lied. So he stopped asking the calendar to hold a heroic month. He asked it to hold three boxes and one walk.

On Sunday night he sent Mrs. Patel a message he had already decided: he would take the spare room, and he would bring the deposit in the morning. The room was part of the sleep box. A quieter house was not a luxury in this plan. It was a tool.

He did not add a new goal after that. Adding was how the page became a wish. He closed the notebook, set both alarms, and put the phone in the kitchen so the bed could not negotiate. The month would not be perfect. It might be repeatable. For Owen, repeatable was the higher prize.

## 11　The Date Slips

- 難度：中等
- 詞數：555
- 主題：會議 × 專案延期
- 聽力焦點：延期的原因，以及主管如何改承諾
- 題旨：週三會議上，印刷廠來不及，菜單週五出不了。Dana 不準大家假裝週五仍可行。她把對外說法改成下週二，並要 Owen 只對客戶講已確認的日期。

### 聽力稿

The Wednesday meeting was longer than Monday's ten minutes, because a date had slipped and dates make people speak in circles. Dana asked everyone to close extra tabs. On the screen was a simple line: menu cards, Friday delivery, café reopening special on Saturday. Mina had drawn a small star next to Friday. The star was now a problem.

**Mina:** The printer called at eight. Their color press is down. They can hand me a proof tonight, but the stack of cards cannot leave their shop before Monday afternoon. If I promise Friday, I am lying to Lena.

Lena was not in the room. She was downstairs, serving the lunch she still had to serve, special or not. Chris, who had only come to report the hallway light, stayed because Dana asked the whole table to hear the new date once. Owen kept his hands off the keyboard. He had learned that typing during a change made people think the change was already published.

Dana let the silence sit until it became useful.

**Dana:** Friday is no longer a date we say out loud. Saturday's special can still happen. It does not need printed cards. It needs soup, a spoken welcome, and a chalkboard. Mina, what do you need from us so Monday's cards are real, not hopeful?

Mina said she needed the final soup name by Thursday noon, a photo she already had, and nobody editing the sentence after the proof. Owen offered to freeze the label text the way he froze client totals, with a note at the top: no changes after noon. Dana accepted that.

Victor from the study group was not here. This was a different kind of shortcut. The shortcut would have been to leave Friday on the poster in the window and "fix it if anyone asked." Dana named that choice and closed it.

**Dana:** If a customer photographs Friday and we open with a chalkboard, we look careless. Careless is more expensive than a delayed card. Owen, when you update the internal note, write Tuesday as the card day. If a client or a partner asks, you give Tuesday. You do not say "probably Friday" to be kind. Kind and unclear is how we got this star.

Owen wrote the sentence before he trusted his memory. Chris asked, almost shyly, whether the chalkboard needed a light. Dana said the third-floor light was his victory, and the café board already had a lamp. People laughed once, then returned to the list.

They spent the rest of the meeting on owners of tasks. Mina owned the proof. Lena would own the soup name, and Mina would call her before the rush ended. Owen owned the wording freeze. Dana owned the apology to the Saturday regulars, which she said she would give in person, not in a caption. The meeting ended when every name had a verb.

Owen stayed one minute to delete Friday from the slide. He did not enjoy deleting. He enjoyed the fact that the new sentence could be repeated by anyone at the table without a private exception. Downstairs, the lunch noise rose and fell. The special would still be a bowl of soup. It would simply not be a stack of cards. That was a smaller promise, and it was one the room could keep.

## 12　What the Rent Has to Cover

- 難度：中等
- 詞數：534
- 主題：出租投資 × 客戶提問
- 聽力焦點：客戶要的數字，以及房仲不肯給的保證
- 題旨：客戶 Helen Cho 想買 Lena 那間小廚房的房子來出租。她只問「一年能賺多少」。Priya 把稅、空屋、維修分開講，並拒絕保證報酬。

### 聽力稿

Helen Cho arrived with a notebook that was cleaner than Owen's and a question she had already practiced. She wanted the small house with the honest sink, the one where Chris would tile a single wall. She did not want to live there. She wanted a tenant, a transfer from her salary account, and a number she could compare with a bond her brother kept mentioning.

Priya had made tea she did not push. She opened a one-page sheet and turned it so Helen could read it.

**Helen:** If I pay the asking price and rent it at the figure in the listing, what do I earn in a year? I do not need a speech. I need the number I can take to my brother.

**Priya:** You can take a range. You should not take a promise. The listing rent is what a careful tenant might pay this spring. It is not a law. If the house sits empty for one month, the year changes. If the tap weeps and becomes a pipe, the year changes again.

Helen frowned in a way that was not rude. She was used to dashboards. Priya drew four lines under the rent. The first line was the loan payment, a guess until a lender confirmed it. The second was tax and insurance. The third was a repair allowance, small every month so a wall did not become a surprise. The fourth was vacancy, one month in twelve, which Priya called a seat belt rather than a prediction.

**Priya:** If you ignore the last two lines, the house looks clever. If you keep them, it looks like a job. Some clients want the clever version. I will not be the person who hands it to you.

Helen asked whether Lena's tenant plan changed the rent. Priya said Lena was not the future tenant. Lena was the seller, and the tenant Lena hoped to find was a separate conversation that might fail. Helen should underwrite the house, not Lena's hope. That sentence landed. Helen wrote "underwrite the house, not the hope" and underlined hope twice.

They walked through the kitchen. The wall Chris would open was marked with a pencil date. Helen asked if she could condition the offer on the wall being finished and dry. Priya said yes, that was a normal condition, and it was different from asking the seller to promise a profit. A dry wall could be inspected. A profit could not.

**Helen:** My brother will say I am slow.

**Priya:** Slow is allowed. If he wants a single number, give him the rent, then show him the four lines. If he still wants a guarantee, that guarantee is not something this house can sign.

Helen did not make an offer that afternoon. She asked for the sheet by email and for the name of the lender Owen had heard about in another meeting, only if Priya was comfortable sharing a name without a referral fee she had not disclosed. Priya said she would send two lender names and no favorite. Helen thanked her for the missing favorite. ## 13　A Ladder and One Share

- 難度：中等
- 詞數：489
- 主題：債券梯 × 單一股票，朋友辯論
- 聽力焦點：兩種工具的差別，誰在承擔什麼

### 聽力稿

**Farid:** The sponsor's brand is on the car I like. If the team wins, the company looks strong, people talk, the share rises. I am not a child. I know it can fall. I still want one clear bet, not a pile of boring paper.

**Owen:** A pile can be a ladder. You buy bonds, or very short funds that behave like them, that mature on different months. When one step matures, you get cash back and you decide again. You are not clever. You are scheduled. The stock is the opposite. You may be right about the team and still lose if the company is expensive, or if a factory burns, or if the win was already in the price.

Farid spun a bottle cap. He said Owen sounded like a pamphlet. Owen admitted the pamphlet problem and tried again with a bus fare. If Farid needed the money for rent in June, a share that "should" rise by August was the wrong tool, even if the story was beautiful. If Farid could leave the money alone for years and could watch it drop by half without selling in anger, the share was a possible tool. The ladder was for money that had a date. The share was for money that had a stomach.

**Farid:** So you are not telling me no.

**Owen:** I am telling you to name the pain before you buy. Write the sentence: "If this falls thirty percent, I will hold, or I will sell." If you cannot finish the sentence, you do not have a plan. You have a cap and a hope.

They did not open a brokerage page. Farid wrote the sentence on the back of the race map and left the verb blank on purpose. He said he would fill "hold" or "sell" after a night of sleep, not after a highlight clip. Owen felt the ethics page in his pocket like a small stone. Farid was a friend, not a client. Still, a friend deserved the same pause. Owen had not said "you should buy." He had said "a date and a stomach are different jobs."

Before Farid locked the door, he asked one more thing. If the team lost and the share rose anyway, who was wrong? Owen said maybe nobody. The car is not the company. Farid groaned, then laughed, because the sentence ruined a neat story and saved him from a neat mistake. They agreed to look at the blank verb on Sunday. Until then the money stayed in the plain account that paid almost nothing and did not pretend to be a race.

## 15　After the Presentation

He showed a chart of hours, with ranges, not totals that belonged to anyone else. He explained why Friday had been removed from the menu project. He ended with a sentence he had not practiced: "So the team should stop taking extra shifts in exam month."

The sentence hung in the air like a flag he had not meant to raise. Mina looked at her hands. Dana waited until the slides were off.

**Dana:** Come with me. Not because you failed. Because the last sentence needs a smaller coat.

They stood by the window, not in front of the others. Dana praised the order. He had said what the chart was, what it was not, and where the numbers came from. He had not decorated uncertainty. That was the part she wanted him to keep.

**Dana:** The last line moved you from reporter to boss. You are not the boss of Mina's shifts, and I did not ask you to be. "Should" is a door. If you open it, you own the room behind it. **Owen:** If you do want a rule later, how should I bring it?

## 16　The Price of the Latte

- 難度：中等
- 詞數：530
- 主題：小店定價 × 常客
- 聽力焦點：漲價理由、誰會受傷、折衷
- 題旨：豆子漲價，Lena 想把大杯調漲。常客 Victor 覺得常客不該先被罰。兩人同意大杯漲、小杯維持兩週，並把原因寫在黑板而不是只改收銀機。

### 聽力稿

The invoice arrived on a Tuesday, which Lena later called a rude day for invoices. Coffee beans were up. Milk was up by less, but milk was in almost every cup. She spread the paper on the counter after the morning rush and did the sum twice, hoping for a softer result. The second sum was the same. If she kept the large latte at the old price, she would be paying customers to drink it by August.

Victor was at the window table with a book he was not reading. He had become a regular in the way regulars do, by being there often enough to have an opinion about the mugs. Lena decided to try the sentence on a human before she tried it on a chalkboard.

**Lena:** I am going to raise the large latte. Not the espresso. The large one is where the milk is. I would rather say it here than surprise you at the machine.

**Victor:** I figured the beans were guilty. My objection is not the math. My objection is the order. Regulars find out by being charged. Tourists find out by reading a board they expected. If you tell the room, I will grumble and stay. If you only tell the register, I will grumble and think you hid.

Lena had planned a quiet register change, precisely to avoid a grumble. Victor's version of respect was noisier and, she admitted, cleaner. She asked what would make the change feel like a decision instead of a pinch. He said two weeks of warning, the reason in one line, and a size that did not move, so a person who was counting coins had a door left open.

She looked at the small cup. Leaving it still would squeeze her margin on purpose. It would also keep the student who bought one small drink and occupied a corner with a library book. Owen was not the only one. She could carry that cost for a short window. She could not carry every size forever.

**Lena:** Large goes up on the first of next month. Small stays until the fifteenth, then I look again. The board will say: beans cost more, large cup changes, small cup waits. If you hate it, tell me before the first, not after you have paid.

Victor said he would hate it a little and tell her anyway, which she accepted as loyalty. She wrote the line on the board in the afternoon, before she could sand it down into something vague. Two customers asked questions. One switched to small immediately. One said she was glad the shop was not pretending milk was free. Lena counted that as a fair split.

At closing she put the invoice in a folder marked "reasons," not "bad news." Owen, wiping the last table, read the board and nodded the way he nodded at a clear rule in a rented room. A price that can be said out loud is easier to live beside than a price that only appears on a receipt. Lena turned the sign to closed and felt poorer and more honest, which was an odd pair and a workable one.

## 21　The Saturday Committee

- 難度：強化
- 詞數：967
- 主題：投資組合 × 出租屋 × 客戶會議
- 聽力焦點：單一街道的房子已經是賭注；基金不是保證
- 題旨：週六，Helen 同時想買那間小房子，又想把一筆錢放進平衡型基金。Dana 主持，Owen 只報告情境。會議沒有成交，只留下三個可以檢驗的情境，以及 Owen 必須揭露自己是房客、不是這位客戶的顧問。

### 聽力稿

Saturday meetings were not a habit Dana loved, but Helen could not leave her weekday desk, and the house would not pause its calendar for anyone's convenience. They used the small room above the bookstore, the same table that rocked, now steadied with a folded napkin. Owen sat at the side with a memo that had two headings he had printed in bold: Observation and Suggestion. Dana had made the headings a condition of his being in the room.

Helen put her notebook square to the edge. She had the rental sheet Priya had built, and she had a fund fact sheet her brother had circled with a pen hard enough to dent the paper.

**Helen:** I can buy the house, or I can put this cash in the fund, or I can do a portion of each. My brother says the fund is diversified and the house is a toy. I am not here for his adjective. I am here to see whether doing both is a plan or a pile.

Dana asked Owen to start with what the paper could support. He kept his voice in the observation coat.

**Owen:** The house is one building on one street. If a bus route changes, or a pipe fails, or the tenant leaves, the effect is local and large. The fund holds many securities. A bad week in one company is a smaller cut. That is not a claim that the fund will rise. It is a claim about how pain is spread. The house also asks for cash at awkward times: a repair, a month with no rent, a tax date. The fund, as this fact sheet describes it, can be sold in pieces, but selling in a bad week locks in that week's price.

Helen asked for the number again, the yearly earn. Owen did not give it. He gave three scenes, which Dana had approved in advance. In the first, the tenant stayed, the wall remained dry, and the fund had an ordinary year. The household cash felt boring and sufficient. In the second, the house sat empty for six weeks and the fund dropped at the same time Helen wanted to pay for a new pipe. She would be selling the fund at the moment it was least willing to help. In the third, the house was fine and the fund was fine and she still felt poor, because she had counted the listing rent as salary and forgotten the repair line Priya had drawn.

**Dana:** Notice what Owen did not say. He did not say which scene is likely. If we pretend to know the likelihood to the nearest percent, we are decorating. Helen, your brother called the house a toy. A toy is something you can ignore. A mortgage is not that. If you buy it, it becomes a large single bet sitting beside a fund you bought in order to avoid large single bets. That tension is the decision. The fund does not cancel the street. It only gives you a second pocket.

Helen was quiet in the way Noor was quiet in an attic and Lena was quiet before pricing a soup. She did not like the phrase second pocket. She repeated it anyway.

**Helen:** If I buy the house with most of the cash, the fund becomes a decoration. If I buy a smaller share of the house, I may not buy it at all, because the seller wants a clean offer. So the real choice is house now, fund later, or fund now, house never. "Both" is a sentence that hides a sequence.

Owen glanced at Dana, who nodded permission to agree with an observation. He said Helen had just described timing, not a product. Dana added the professional edge Owen was not allowed to blur. Helen was Dana's client. Owen was in the room as a junior note-taker on a training seat. He rented a room from Mrs. Patel. He did not represent the seller, the lender, or the fund. If Helen wanted a name struck from the memo because he was a tenant in the same city, she could say so. Helen said the city was large and the disclosure was enough, provided it stayed written.

They argued, politely, about the repair allowance. Helen wanted it smaller because Chris had already looked at the wall. Dana wanted it kept, because a contractor's glance was not a twenty-year roof. Owen read back the three scenes with the allowance still inside them. Helen did not sign an offer. She asked for the memo by evening, with the headings intact, and she asked Dana to be the only person who sent it.

**Dana:** You will not get a recommendation that says "buy." You will get the scenes and a list of what would make us revisit them: a finished roof repair, a loan lock you actually hold, and a fund purchase small enough that an empty month does not force a sale. If your brother wants a slogan, he can write it himself. I will not put our names under it.

After Helen left, Owen stayed to level the napkin under the table leg. Dana asked him which coat he had worn. He said observation, except for one moment when he had wanted to say "do not buy both at full size." She said the want was human and the silence was the job. He added the disclosure about Mrs. Patel to the top of the memo before he trusted himself to remember it later. The Saturday light on the bookstore floor was the same shape as the light in Noor's empty living room. He noticed it, and he did not put it in the memo. Sunlight was still not a number Andre would lend against.

## 22　The Review She Asked For

- 難度：強化

- 聽力焦點：客戶要的是安慰還是可行動的檢討；顧問如何分開事實與歉意

### 聽力稿

Helen did not bring her brother. She brought the fact sheet with the dented circle and a printout of a month that had not been ordinary. The fund was down. The house offer had never been sent. She sat before Dana invited her to, which Dana allowed, because anger that is already seated will not be improved by a ritual.

**Helen:** You said an ordinary year. This month was not ordinary. I did not buy the house because you made "both" sound childish. Now the thing I did buy is smaller, and I want to know whether you hid the chance of this month inside a soft word.

Dana poured water and did not pour a speech on top of it. She asked Owen to read, from the Saturday memo, the sentence about likelihood. He found it. They had refused to assign a percent. They had named a bad week as a scene, not as a surprise that should be impossible. Helen listened with her arms folded, then unfolded them, which Owen took as a mechanical change and not yet a forgiveness.

**Dana:** "Ordinary" was the wrong coat. I used it to mean "we will not forecast a hero." You heard it as "the line will stay near zero." Those are different promises. I can apologize for the coat. I cannot apologize for the month as if the month broke a guarantee we signed. We did not sign one. If I pretend we did, I am buying your calm with a lie, and the next month will send you back angrier.

Helen asked what an apology was worth if the balance was still lower. Dana said an apology was worth clarity, not cash. Cash would be a performance. If the firm repaid every down month, it would be selling insurance without the premium or the contract, and it would favor the client who complained over the client who did not. That was not a standard she would invent on a Thursday.

Owen kept his pen moving so his face would not join the negotiation. Dana asked Helen to restate her actual goal, because a review without a goal becomes a trial of adjectives. Helen was quiet for longer than a polite meeting enjoys.

**Helen:** I wanted the money to be there if the house's pipe failed, or if I left my job. I did not want a story about a sponsor or a street. The month scared me because I imagined needing the money during the scare. I did not need it. I acted as if I did.

**Owen:** The memo's second scene was that picture. Empty house, fund down, a repair due. You are not in that scene. You are in a scene where the fund is down and the repair is not due. The feeling can match a scene you are not in.

Dana let the sentence stand. It was observation, and it was close to the bone, and Helen did not swat it away. She asked whether she should sell now to "stop the bleeding," a phrase she apologized for even as she used it. Dana refused the medical costume. Selling would turn a printed loss into a finished loss. Holding would keep the possibility of a further fall. Neither verb was holy. The right verb depended on whether Helen's job and her emergency cash still matched the goal she had just said aloud.

They built a smaller test. Helen would keep six months of expenses in the plain account Andre would have called boring. She would not use the fund for a mood. She would reread the scene list before any sale above a modest size, and she would call Dana before the click, not after. Dana wrote "call before the click" on the fact sheet, beside the dented circle, and Helen initialed the ink, not as a contract of profit, but as a contract of sequence.

**Helen:** If you had said "this can drop hard and still be the tool" in those words, I would have argued, and I might have bought less. I would rather have argued in September than in this chair.

**Dana:** Then we will use those words next time, even if they cost us a polite hour. Owen, add that sentence to her file as my wording, not as a forecast. And Helen, if you want a different adviser, I will help the handoff without a speech about loyalty. Loyalty is not a substitute for fit.

Helen said she would stay for one more quarter and judge the clarity, not the month. Owen typed the line before the meeting could soften it. When she left, the rocking table wobbled again. Dana put the napkin back under the leg. She told Owen that a review is successful when the client can repeat her own goal without borrowing the adviser's adjectives. Helen had done that. The fund had not apologized. It was not required to. The humans in the room were required to speak in coats that matched the job, and on that narrower test, the hour had earned its discomfort.

## 24　Numbers on the Second Page

- 難度：強化
- 詞數：852
- 主題：財務報表 × 主管追問

- 題旨：Owen 用一家虛構的小租賃公司做練習簡報。帳上有獲利，現金卻變緊，因為裝修先付钱、租金後進帳。Dana 逼他指出哪一行會讓房東在獲利的月份仍然睡不好。

### 聽力稿

Dana had given Owen a practice company, not a client. The company owned three small apartments and a habit of improving them just before the rent increased. The numbers were invented, clean enough to teach and messy enough to bruise. Owen stood at the same end of the table where Dana refused to sit like a monarch. Mina was there as a witness, not as a designer. The point of a witness was to stop him from narrating to a mirror.

He began well. Page one was profit. Rent came in. Expenses went out. The year showed a gain. He said the gain slowly, then turned the page. Page two was cash. **Dana:** Stop on that sentence. Say it again without the word "still," which is doing too much comfort.

**Owen:** The company can show a profit in a season when the cash is tight, because the improvement is paid before the new rent arrives.

**Dana:** Better. Now point to the line that would keep the owner awake. Not the line that makes the year look successful. The line that makes a Tuesday difficult.

He pointed to the March cash drop, then to a line he had almost buried: a deposit from a tenant that the company was holding and did not own. Mina asked, from the side, whether a deposit felt like cash you could use. Owen said it felt like it, and that feeling was the trap. The deposit was a liability wearing a bank balance as a costume. If the tenant left and the wall was fine, the money would have to go back. An owner who spent it on tile was borrowing from the tenant without the tenant's signature.

Dana asked him to connect the picture to Lena's hallway without using Lena's name, since practice numbers should not drag a real landlord into a fictional profit. He said a person can be "ahead" on paper while a fan is still in a truck and a plumber is still owed. The paper is not a lie. It is a different clock. People get hurt when they pay Wednesday's bills with December's story.

**Mina:** If I were the owner, I would have told my cousin the profit number and forgotten the deposit. That is the slide that needs the ugly label.

Owen added a box in the speaker notes: "Deposit is not spending money." He almost added "the owner should wait to renovate." He heard Dana's earlier lesson and labeled the next box as a suggestion, in a different color, and he said the label aloud before he said the idea. The suggestion was to schedule improvements after a cash buffer, not after a mood of optimism. Dana did not adopt it as policy. She said the suggestion was allowed to exist because it was wearing a name tag.

They spent the last minutes on questions a client like Helen might ask. Would a loan fix the March hole? Owen said a loan would move the hole from the plumber to a future payment, which might be wise or merely delayed, depending on the interest and the certainty of the June rent. He did not pick wise. Dana nodded at the restraint. Mina asked how anyone slept. Owen said they slept by keeping page two on top of page one in the drawer they actually opened. Mina asked for a minute with a calculator, not to catch him, but to see the hole in rent-weeks. Owen let her say the ugly sum out loud. That was what a witness was for. Dana added that a relative's transfer would not arrive as profit, and it would not arrive as free cash. It would arrive as another clock, with a payment on a birthday every month. Owen wrote "a relative is a clock" in the margin and saved his smile for the hallway.

After the room cleared, Owen filed the practice set in a folder named "clocks," not "answers." He wrote Helen's name nowhere on it. He wrote, at the bottom, the sentence Dana had made him repeat. Profit and cash share a year and do not share a Tuesday. He thought of Mrs. Patel's rent, due on the first, and of the lemon he was not to strip from the branch. Even a kind house ran on a clock. The job of the second page was to keep kindness from spending money that still belonged to someone else.

## 25　A Floor Under the Price

- 難度：強化
- 詞數：811
- 主題：選擇權避險 × 客戶面談
- 聽力焦點：保護下跌要付成本；顧問不肯保證地板
- 題旨：Farid 的表親 Paul 在 Dana 的客戶名單上，持有那家贊助商股票，想在比賽月前「設一個地板」。Dana 解釋買進保護的成本與限制。Owen 在場做紀錄。沒有人答應股價不會跌。

### 聽力稿

Paul was older than Farid and less romantic about cars, which did not stop him from owning a block of the sponsor's shares he had bought years before the current team existed. He had come because the race month made the price loud, and loud prices gave him ideas. The idea had a nickname. He wanted a floor.

Dana asked him what the nickname meant before she let anyone attach a product to it. Paul said he wanted the right to sell the shares at a price he could live with, if the month went badly, and he wanted to keep the shares if the month went well. He had read enough to know the instrument had a cost. He had not decided whether the cost was a fee or a punishment.

**Dana:** The tool you are describing is a put option, a contract that gives you the right to sell at a chosen price before a chosen date. You pay for that right up front. If the share stays above that price, the right expires and the money you paid is gone. That is not a failure of the tool. That is the tool working as insurance you did not need to claim. If the share falls through the floor, the contract pays you in the way the contract says, which is not the same sentence as "my life feels the same as last year."

Owen wrote "insurance you did not claim is still a cost" and did not decorate it. Paul asked whether the firm would choose the floor price for him. Dana said they could show how different floors changed the premium, the way different deductibles changed an ordinary policy. They would not choose his stomach for him. A higher floor cost more, because it was more likely to be touched. A lower floor cost less and let more pain through before it helped. There was no setting called "only the bad part of the fall, for free."

**Paul:** Farid talks as if the car is the company. I do not believe that. I do believe a noisy month makes me click. I want the clicks to be harder.

**Dana:** Then we should talk about a rule as well as a contract. A contract spends cash to build a floor. A rule spends nothing and fails the first night you are tired. Some clients use both: a modest floor, and a written promise to call us before any extra trade during the race weeks. The call is not magic. It inserts a person between you and the button.

Owen thought of Helen's "call before the click" and kept the parallel out of his mouth. Different client, different file. He did ask, because Dana had told him questions were safer than speeches, whether Paul needed the shares' value for a dated bill. Paul said no. The shares were not rent. They were a position he could imagine holding for years, and also a position he could imagine ruining in one televised weekend. Dana said that sentence was the best risk report he had given all month.

They looked at three example premiums, round numbers, clearly marked as examples that would be stale by the afternoon. Paul disliked all three and then disliked his dislike, which Dana said was a healthy sign. He asked what the firm earned. Dana told him, in figures, the commission and the absence of any payment from the sponsor. She said if a race-weekend promotion ever offered the firm a prize for opening option tickets, he would hear it from her before a headline, and she would decline it. Owen typed the disclosure while she spoke so the sentence could not shrink on the way to the file.

**Paul:** I will not buy the floor today. I will write the rule tonight: no orders from the sofa while a race is on. If I still want the contract next week, I will ask for fresh premiums and I will pick the deductible myself. If Farid tells you I was brave, correct him. I was slow.

Dana said slow was a strategy with a clock, and she did not smile too much at her own echo of a lesson she had not taught him. After Paul left, Owen filed the examples in a sleeve marked "stale by design." He did not text Farid. The cousin's account was not a kitchen-table story. On the bus he watched a highlight on someone else's phone, sound off, and felt the old itch to explain pit stops. He let the itch pass. A floor under a price was not a tire. It was a paid right with an expiry, and it did not care who won the corner. That indifference was the point he wanted to remember the next time a client arrived with a nickname for fear.

## 28　The Debrief and the Sponsor

- 難度：強化
- 詞數：854
- 主題：F1 賽後檢討 × 贊助商會議
- 聽力焦點：工程語言如何被翻譯成贊助商聽得懂的取捨
- 題旨：車手 Leo 早進站後卡在車陣裡。工程師 Imani 在檢討會上堅持這是位置與抓地的交換，不是勇氣問題。接著贊助商 Sable 要一句能放進社群的話。Imani 拒絕把策略說成注定的勝利。

### 聽力稿

The garage after a finish is a loud room pretending to be a quiet one. Tools went back into drawers. A wheel blanket lay on the floor like a spent flag. Leo sat on a stool with his gloves off and his eyes still driving. Imani stood by the screen, which had been rewound to the pit window and then paused, because she did not trust a moving picture to keep people honest.

**Imani:** We pitted on lap eighteen because the tire model said the cliff was close and the gap behind was large enough to rejoin without a disaster. The rejoin was clean. The next five laps were not. We sat in dirty air behind a slower car, and we did not pass. The strategy bought grip we then spent in traffic. That is a cost. It is not a confession that the window was imaginary.

Leo wanted the pass to have happened. He said the car behind him on track, the one they had jumped by pitting, looked beatable and then was not, because its tires were older but its position was a wall. Imani agreed with the wall. She did not agree that an earlier or later stop would have been free. Later would have been a bet that the cliff was a rumor. Earlier would have been a bet against a safety car that never came. She wrote the three bets on the whiteboard in verbs: pit, stay, pray. She crossed out pray in front of him, not as a joke against faith, but as a refusal to file hope under engineering.

**Leo:** When I say on the radio that we are stuck, I am not asking for a poem. I am asking for a gap that is not there. If the next time you tell me to box, I need to know you have already hated the traffic as much as I will.

**Imani:** Then the call will include the ugly sentence. "You will come out behind, and the pass is not promised." If you want only calls that feel like attacks, you will get fewer calls, and some of the cliffs will arrive with your name on them. I can live with your anger. I cannot live with a radio that only sells good news.

They watched the five laps once, sound low. Leo pointed at the moment the slower car covered the inside. Imani pointed at the lap time three circuits earlier, where their own tire had given up a tenth it never returned. Both fingers were right. A debrief that crowns one finger is how teams start lying by Thursday. They agreed on a test for the next race: if the model showed a cliff inside four laps and the rejoin gap was under two seconds, the default would be stay out unless Leo confirmed he wanted the traffic. The default was a rule. The confirmation was a human lock. It looked, though neither of them knew it, like Paul's sofa rule, a click delayed by a second voice.

Sable arrived as the rule was still wet on the board. She handled the sponsor's hospitality and the sentences that left the garage. She had a phone and a deadline and a smile that had survived worse afternoons.

**Sable:** I need one line for the feed. Not a novel. Something like "bold strategy, unlucky traffic, proud of the fight." People understand fight. They do not understand a tire model.

**Imani:** "Bold" is a costume. We were not bold. We were early relative to a cliff, and we paid in position. You can say: "We changed tires to protect the second half of the race and spent the next laps behind a slower car. The pass did not come." That is a line a driver can hear in a month without flinching. If the feed needs pride, it can be proud of the stop itself. The stop was quick. The story after it was ordinary. Ordinary is allowed to be published.

Sable read it back, grimacing at "ordinary," then kept it, because Imani would not sign a synonym that meant "we were robbed." Leo added that the crew's stop time could be praised without praise for his afternoon. Sable said that split would please the crew and bore the comments, and she would take the boredom. Before she posted, Imani asked to see the text on the screen, not in a paraphrase. One word had already tried to become "fearless." She deleted it herself. Fearless was how pray had attempted to re-enter the building.

Later, in the lot, Leo thanked her in a sentence with no camera on it. He said the ugly radio line would make him calmer, not braver. Imani said calm was the product. Bravery was a headline other people could have. She drove home along the river with the whiteboard phrase still in her head: pit, stay, and the verb she had crossed out. A season was just a long argument with that list. Anyone who wanted a shorter list was welcome to sell tickets. They were not welcome to write her call.

## 29　Borrow, Sell, or Wait

- 難度：強化
- 詞數：861
- 主題：公司金融 × 三間出租屋
- 聽力焦點：借錢、賣掉一間、或延後裝修，三種時鐘
- 題旨：Lena 和姊姊 Amina 名下有三間小房。會計師說帳上賺錢、現金緊。銀行員願意貸一筆裝修款。Amina 想賣掉最吵的一間。會議把「能還的貸」和「想做的牆」拆開，決定只修一間、不賣、貸款額度縮到第一間的工程。

### 聽力稿

The three apartments were not an empire. They were a family argument with roofs. Lena had bought the first with café savings. Amina had inherited the second from an uncle who loved bus routes. Together they had stretched for the third, the noisy one, because the price had looked like a sale and the price had been telling the truth about the buses. Now all three needed something: a wall, a boiler, a window that hummed. The café invoice for beans sat in a different folder and somehow in the same mood.

Their accountant, Mr. Idris, came with page two on top, which made Owen's practice company feel less like homework and more like a neighbor. He was not Owen. He did not know Owen. He simply did not trust profit to pay a plumber.

**Mr. Idris:** The year shows a gain. The bank account shows a season. March paid for work. June's higher rent is a story you are spending in April. The deposits you hold are not a renovation fund. If you spend them, you are borrowing from tenants who think their money is asleep, not tiled to a wall.

Amina had come prepared to sell the noisy apartment. She had a price in her head and a silence she wanted at dinner. Lena had come prepared to borrow, because selling felt like admitting the stretch had been a costume. The banker, Ms. Ruiz, was prepared to do neither person's feelings. She had a term sheet with a blank where the amount should be.

**Ms. Ruiz:** I can lend against the calmer two, not against a story that rents always rise when rates do. The payment I would add has to fit in the year you are having, not the year your brother-in-law describes at holidays. If the loan only works when every unit is full and every wall is quiet, it does not work. Tell me the project you can still finish if one unit is empty for a month.

Lena named the kitchen wall that had already taught her a fan's worth of humility, a different building, already handled, and then she corrected herself. For this portfolio the urgent job was the boiler in the first apartment, the one a tenant actually shivered in. The humming window could wait for a summer. The noisy apartment could keep being noisy and rented. Amina hated the wait. She said a wait was a decision to keep a mistake in the family because selling had a tax and a mood.

Mr. Idris put the tax on a corner of the page without letting it become the boss. A sale would bring cash and a bill, and it would end the bus noise, and it would also end a rent that, even discounted for the street, paid a real share of the loan they already had. Selling the problem child might improve dinner and worsen the calendar. He would not call either outcome virtue.

**Lena:** Then we borrow only what the boiler and its honest margin require. We do not borrow a dream of three new kitchens. Amina, if you still hate the noisy one in December, we look at a sale with the tax written in the first sentence, not the last. Ms. Ruiz, size the loan to the boiler. If your payment does not fit a month with one empty unit, shrink it again. I would rather shiver through one more planning night than sign a payment that needs perfect luck.

Ms. Ruiz recalculated while they argued about a lemon cake in the café, a mercy topic. The smaller loan fit, barely, inside the empty-month test. Amina initialed the decision she disliked and added a review date in December, which Mr. Idris said was the most adult thing on the page. Lena called Chris, not for a new empire, but for one boiler and a refusal to discuss tile. He sounded relieved. Empires were how small contractors learned to fear their best clients.

Amina asked what they would tell the tenant in the cold apartment that night. Lena said the truth would be the first sentence, not the third: a boiler was coming, the date followed Ms. Ruiz's papers, and a space heater was a bridge, not a personality. Mr. Idris told them to put the heater's power cost into the empty-month test if the owner was paying the bill. Lena added a small number. The loan still fit. Barely stayed barely. She would rather see barely on a term sheet than meet it later in a group chat full of cousins.

In the evening Lena priced the large latte again in her head and did not change it. The bean invoice and the boiler were cousins. Both asked her to say a limit out loud. She wrote the December date on the café whiteboard's back, where customers could not see it and she could not forget it. Amina texted a picture of the noisy street with no caption. Lena replied with the review date and nothing comforting. Comfort would have been a second loan. They had agreed, for this season, to live without it.

### 中文翻譯

三間公寓不是帝國，是有屋頂的家庭爭論。Lena 用咖啡館存款買第一間；Amina 從愛公車路線的叔叔繼承第二間；她們一起買第三間——吵的那間，價格像特價、公車路線說的是實話。現在三間都要一點：牆、鍋爐、嗡嗡窗。豆子發票在別資料夾，心情在同一處。

會計 Mr. Idris 把第二頁放最上——Owen 的練習公司從作業變鄰居。他不認識 Owen，只不信獲利能付 plumber。

**Mr. Idris：** 這年帳上賺；銀行是季節。三月付工程，六月較高租金是四月就花掉的故事。代管押金不是裝修基金——花掉等於向以為錢在睡覺、不是被貼上牆的房客借錢。

Mr. Idris 把稅放頁角，不讓它當老闆：賣有現金也有帳單，結束公車噪音也結束一份租金——即使街道打過折，仍付她們既有貸款真實的一份。賣問題兒可能改善晚餐、惡化日曆；他不把任一結果叫美德。

**Lena：** 那只借鍋爐加老實緩衝需要的量，不借三間新廚房夢。Amina，十二月若仍討厭吵間，再看出售，稅寫第一句。Ms. Ruiz，貸款縮到鍋爐；月付若放不進空屋月，再縮。我寧願再發抖計畫一晚，也不簽需要完美運氣的月付。

Ms. Ruiz 在她們爭檸檬蛋糕時重算——慈悲話題。較小貸款剛好過空屋月測試。Amina 在不喜歡的決定上簽縮寫，加十二月覆核日；Mr. Idris 說那是這頁最成人的一行。Lena 打 Chris——不是新帝國，是鍋爐，且拒談磁磚；他聽起來鬆口氣。帝國是小包商學會怕最好客戶的方式。

Amina 問那晚跟冷公寓房客說什麼。Lena 說真話第一句：鍋爐要來，日期跟 Ms. Ruiz 文件，電暖器是橋不是人格。Mr. Idris 說若屋主付帳，電暖器電費要進空屋月測試。Lena 加小數，貸款仍放得下——剛好仍是剛好；她寧在條款單上看見剛好，也不要晚點在表親群組遇見。

晚上 Lena 腦裡又算大杯拿鐵價，沒改。豆子發票和鍋爐是表親，都要她把限度說出聲。她把十二月日期寫在咖啡館白板背面——客人看不見，她不會忘。Amina 傳吵街照片無說明；Lena 只回覆核日，不安慰——安慰會像第二筆貸。這季她們同意不靠它過。

## 30　The Week in One Notebook

- 難度：強化
- 詞數：965
- 主題：總整：生活、職場、房產、客戶、風險
- 聽力焦點：一個星期裡同一個標準如何換上不同的外套
- 題旨：Owen 用一本筆記走過一週。鬧鐘與湯、延期的菜單、Noor 的釘子、Paul 的沙發、Helen 的三個情境，最後都收成同一種句子：先說時鐘，再說誰受益，不把希望寫成保證。週日他把筆記讀給 Farid 聽，但沒有說出任何客戶的名字。

### 聽力稿

The notebook's right-hand page had been three boxes. By Sunday it was a week, which is a box that has learned to walk. Owen sat in Mrs. Patel's yard, lemon branch untouched, and read only the lines he was allowed to say to another person. Farid was the other person. The condition, stated first, was that names of clients would stay ink, not air.

Monday had been the ten-minute meeting and the chart that did not deserve exact totals. He told Farid the shape: a colleague stopped before sharing figures that were not yet released, and a manager praised the stop. Farid said that sounded like refusing to pit before the window. Owen said it was closer to refusing to invent a window. They let the metaphor limp. Not every pause was a tire.

Tuesday was the café and the second alarm and the board about the large latte. He could talk about the latte. Lena had written the reason in public. Beans cost more. The large cup would change. The small cup would wait. Farid, who counted coins in race months, said he would have wanted the warning too. Owen said the warning was the whole ethics of a price. A receipt is a late meeting.

Wednesday was the slipped Friday. He described a star on a calendar that had to be unnamed because it had started to lie. The room had replaced it with a day everyone could repeat. Farid asked if the special had survived. Owen said the soup had survived and the stack of cards had not, and that survival was the correct ranking. A chalkboard can tell the truth at two-thirty. A printed card that says Friday when the press is dead cannot.

Thursday belonged to a nail and a rate, though he was careful. He did not say Noor's name. He said a friend of a friend had a dry stain that was not a dry roof, and a loan letter that was a corridor rather than a key. The same day, a headline had moved an example payment. The friend had refused to hurry a condition in order to outrun a headline. Farid whistled. He said his own blank verb, hold or sell, was still blank, and the headline had tempted him to fill it with a shout. Owen asked only whether the money had a date. Farid said it did not. Owen said then the headline was noise, and noise was not an order. He did not mention Paul. The sofa trade was a file, not a yard conversation. The principle, dateless money still needs a rule, was as far as friendship could walk.

Friday was the practice company and the real hallway, braided only by a clock. He told Farid that profit and cash can share a year and miss each other on a Tuesday, and that a deposit is a guest, not a wallet. He said a landlord he knew, and here he almost slipped, had chosen not to spend a tenant's quiet. He corrected himself. He said he had seen, in a made-up set of apartments and in a real conversation he would not detail, the same choice: fix the thing that makes someone cold, do not borrow a dream of three perfect kitchens, and write the review date where you can see it and customers cannot. Farid asked if this was about the noisy street they had once walked. Owen said many streets were noisy. Farid accepted the dodge with a grace that made him a better friend than his shortcuts suggested.

Saturday's memo and Sunday's confirmation call folded into one warning Owen could give without a name. A scene list is not a promise that only the pleasant scene will show up. A call you meant to make is not a lock on a button. If you want a lock, you install a lock. If you want a floor under a price, you pay for a floor and you admit the payment is gone when the floor is never touched. Farid said that last part ruined insurance for him. Owen said insurance that felt free was how people came to hate insurers. They sat with that until the lemon leaves moved.

**Farid:** Read me the line you are keeping, the one that is yours and not a client's.

**Owen:** If I am not sure who benefits, I ask before I speak. If a number has two clocks, I say both clocks. If a plan needs luck to pay the plumber, it is not a plan yet. I would rather be slow in a yard than fast on a sofa.

Farid filled his blank verb while Owen looked at the tree. He wrote "hold," then added, "unless the money grows a date." It was not a trade. It was a sentence that could survive a highlight clip. Owen did not praise it too much. Praise made people rewrite. He closed the notebook. The second alarm was already set for Monday, in the kitchen, far from the bed. Mrs. Patel's radio murmured a race replay through an open window and then, mercifully, a weather report. Rain was possible. Rain was not a strategy. It was a condition, and the week had taught him what to do with those: name them, price them, and refuse to tile over them just because a guest is already on the road with boxes.

He walked inside, wiped the counter he had used, and left the shelf as labeled. The page was full. The week was repeatable. For once, full and repeatable were the same thing, and he did not add a fourth box to celebrate. Celebration, he had decided, could be a clean counter and a verb you could still read in the morning.
